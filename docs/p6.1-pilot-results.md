# P6.1 — LLM recall-layer pilot: prompted Qwen 3.5 9B (open scope)

**Date:** 2026-10-12 · **World:** `tier4-v2`, open train+tune scope (sealed test untouched) ·
**Run:** `data/tier4-v2/runs/p6-pilot-1` (`run.json`, `outcomes.jsonl`, `transcript.jsonl`,
`score-open.json`) · **Runner:** `scripts/p6_recall_pilot.py`, prompt `recall-v1`.

## Setup

- Population: every train+tune unit the frozen detector allowed (score < 0.60): 492 units.
  That is 60 abusive (from 4 source groups, spanning at least 9 detector clusters), 408
  legitimate and 24 ambiguous (excluded from metrics). A *source group* is the world author's
  group (its split group); the scorer's `*_clusters` fields count source groups.
- Routing: every allowed unit was sent, a pool that is 87% legitimate. Coinbase routes only a
  selected subset to its agent; this pilot did not.
- Model: `mtplx-qwen35-9b-optimized-speed` on a local MTPLX server, no fine-tuning,
  temperature 0, one sample.
- Prompt context: a compressed history plus per-account summaries. The model never sees the
  detector's score. Appeals are dropped.
- Output: `RISK_LEVEL: 1-10` only. All 492 answers were valid.
- 43 calls first failed with `503 session_busy`, because another client shared the server.
  They were moved to `outcomes-failed-1.jsonl` and retried
  (`p6-pilot-1-retry1.log`, exit 0). The final file has 492 valid rows.

## Result

| Metric (allowed units only) | Value |
|---|---|
| AUC, risk level, abusive vs legitimate (point estimate; units are not independent) | **0.38** |
| AUC within detector band = 0 / (0, 0.4) / [0.4, 0.6) | 0.49 / 0.29 / 0.39 |
| Legitimate units rated ≥ 8 | 275 of 408 (67%) |
| Abusive units rated ≥ 8 | 27 of 60 (45%) |

Per missed source group, as the mean level and its distribution:

| Family | Units | Levels |
|---|---|---|
| Staged household (source group A) | 3 | all 10 |
| Staged household (source group B) | 4 | 8, 9, 9, 9 |
| Direct allocation cycling (source group C) | 32 | mean 4.5: 19 at 1–2, 13 at 8–9 |
| Direct allocation cycling (source group D) | 21 | mean 4.2: 14 at 1–2, 7 at 9–10 |

Flagging at level ≥ 8 would flag 322 of the 492 allowed units: 27 abusive (7
staged-household, 20 direct-allocation-cycling; all 4 missed source groups represented; 1
source group with no detector catch now caught), 275 legitimate and 20 ambiguous. Unit recall would go from 0.70
to 0.84, and the false-positive rate from 0.61 to 0.87. Of the 302 flags with binary labels,
27 (9%) are abuse, below the 12.8% base rate of the labelled allowed pool.

## Reading

1. **Poor ranking on this sample.** The prompted 9B model does not rank missed abuse above
   legitimate allowed units (AUC 0.38). It rates most legitimate allowed units high-risk, and
   it rates most of the dominant missed pattern, direct allocation cycling, as low-risk. Why
   it rates legitimate shared-use units high is not isolated, and
   no input ablation was run. The prompt says sharing is not abuse by itself. It also tells
   the model the detector did not flag the case and asks for abuse the detector may have
   missed, which could push it toward flagging. The world's brief asked for legitimate
   families that look abusive, and abusive families that imitate legitimate sharing.
2. **A dominated trade-off.** All 7 staged-household units and 20 of the 53 cycling units
   were rated 8 or higher: 27 abusive units, alongside 275 legitimate and 20 ambiguous. The
   27 catches are real coverage. But the model flagged 45% of abusive vs 67% of legitimate
   allowed units. A random pick of the same 302 labelled units would catch about
   60 × 302/468 ≈ 39 abusive units on average; a random 322 of all 492 gives about 39 known
   abusive units too. So on this sample the model catches fewer than random selection would
   on average, at the same volume. No cost model was used or needed for this comparison.
3. **Coinbase Part 3** also reports that their unadapted Qwen3.5-9B "performed poorly" on
   their fraud benchmark, and that post-training on known historical outcomes (RLVR) improved
   it. This is a different, synthetic task, not a replication. The analogous next step here
   would be post-training against this project's synthetic outcomes.
4. **Scope and provenance.** Exploratory, open-scope development: one prompt, one model, one
   generated world. The model's inputs are label-free, but the prompt was written after the
   P6.0 headroom check had already read open-scope labels, including which family the misses
   belonged to. The 60 abusive units come from 4 source groups (53 from 2), so the AUC is a
   descriptive point estimate with no interval. This shows a poor result for this
   configuration on this sample; it does not show that an LLM recall layer cannot work.

## Next options

- **Next step:** post-train a small open model with a deterministic, class-balanced reward
  from the ground-truth store, using train only. Select on tune, and check it once, frozen, on
  a fresh and larger repo-blind world with many independent missed source groups. The open
  allowed pool has only 4 missed source groups, so training positives are scarce, and the
  model may learn the world author's tells.
- Optional diagnostic: score the same prompt on detector-flagged units, to separate the model
  from the pool and the prompt.
- A frontier model under the same prompt, as a comparison point (Coinbase Part 2 style).
- Prompt variants (detector features shown, a calibrated base rate), developed on train and
  checked on tune.
